### The two leakage denominators

**Unsupervised leakage** counts covered assets auto-routed to a paid vendor with no
human in the loop. It is **structurally zero**: GATE blocks every money-spending route
at any confidence, so this number cannot be non-zero without a code defect. It is
reported to prove the property holds, not as an achievement.

**Recommended leakage** counts covered assets the system *recommended* paying for, even
though a human reviewed it. This is the number that moves under ablation, and the one
that becomes real leakage once a busy coordinator starts accepting recommendations.
Reporting only the first would overstate the result.

**The adjustment, stated openly.** 1 covered case(s) took a paid route because a
hazard was reported at critical severity, which fires R-01. A gas leak is attended
first and the warranty claim filed afterwards, so that is correct behaviour and not
leakage — but it *is* an adjustment made after seeing the data, so both the raw and
adjusted figures are published. The affected case is inspectable in the case set.

**No τ satisfies all three constraints**, and *why* is the finding. review load bottoms out at 58.3% (at tau=0.00), above the 35% target. Leakage and safety FN are 0 at EVERY tau, so the threshold is not the binding lever — hard gates are.

The cost-of-autonomy curve is **flat** across the whole leakage/safety dimension: 0% leakage and 0 safety false negatives at every τ from 0.00 to 1.00. Autonomy is therefore not governed by the confidence threshold at all — it is governed by the hard gates (safety flag, warranty boundary, ambiguous asset, missing data, cost ceiling, and any money-spending route), which fire regardless of score.

That inverts the obvious roadmap. Tuning τ buys nothing here. The lever is **reducing how often a hard gate legitimately fires** — chiefly registry ambiguity, which is a data-quality problem rather than a model problem. Shipping at maximum gating and fixing the registry is the honest recommendation, and it is the opposite of what a confidence-threshold dashboard would suggest.

## Known limitations

1. **Decision ground truth is tautological with respect to the rules.** `intendedRoute`
   is computed by the same policy the pipeline applies, so E3 measures whether the
   pipeline *feeds* the rules correctly — not whether the rules are right. Rule
   correctness is tested separately in `src/agent/decide.test.ts` against the policy
   document, and reported as a distinct number.
2. **The case set is deliberately skewed.** The warranty boundary band is over-sampled
   ~6× versus reality because that is where money leaks. Absolute rates here are not
   estimates of production rates.
3. **n=60.** Differences of a few percentage points are not significant at this size.
4. **Synthetic reports.** An LLM realizer writes messier prose than a template but is
   not a store manager. Real intake will contain failure modes absent here.
